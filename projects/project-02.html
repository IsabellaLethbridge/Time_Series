Predicting drought in the Western Cape

Section and topic: Section 1 - Linear models for the conditional mean (seasonal ARIMA and dynamic regression).

Type of project: Implementation and model comparison on real-world data. Includes a prewhitened cross-correlation analysis and a composition adjustment for incomplete buoy data.

Aim: To determine whether Theewaterskloof Dam capacity and eastern Pacific sea surface temperature (SST) can be forecast with seasonal ARIMA models, and whether lagged SST, as a leading indicator of El Niño, improves dam capacity forecasts.

Data used:

  • Daily Theewaterskloof Dam levels (% of capacity), January 2000 to January 2026. Source: City of Cape Town Open Data Portal, Dam levels from 2000, https://odp-cctegis.opendata.arcgis.com/.
  • Daily SST (°C) from the seven TAO/TRITON buoys along 95°W, January 2000 to April 2026 (models fitted to January 2000 to October 2021 because of long gaps in the record). Source: NOAA Pacific Marine Environmental Laboratory, https://www.pmel.noaa.gov/tao/drupal/disdel/.

Both series were aggregated to monthly means. Neither dataset contains personal information. Download instructions are in the detailed analysis and the code and data appendix.

Methods used: STL decomposition, ACF/PACF and unit root tests to choose the differencing and candidate orders. Seven seasonal ARIMA candidates selected by rolling-origin cross-validation (with AICc as a check) on the training data only, then evaluated once on a held-out test set against Mean, Naive, Seasonal naive, Drift and ETS(A,N,A). A prewhitened cross-correlation to choose the SST lag, a time series linear model, and a dynamic regression (SARIMAX). Accuracy measured with RMSE, MAE, MAPE and CRPS; residuals checked with Ljung-Box tests.

Key findings:

  • The selected dam capacity model is ARIMA(1,0,0)(0,1,1)[12] (test CRPS = 4.828, ranked 1 of 6 models, against 9.164 for Seasonal naive). Most of the predictability comes from the dam’s own persistence and seasonality.
  • The selected SST model is ARIMA(2,0,0)(0,1,1)[12] (test CRPS = 0.585, ranked 2 of 6). It performed very similarly to the Seasonal naive model on the short SST test set, with a slightly higher CRPS but lower RMSE, MAE and MAPE.
  • The two-year dam capacity forecast keeps the seasonal cycle (monthly means between 43.1% and 77.6%), but the 95% prediction interval widens to about 66 percentage points by the end of the horizon and exceeds 100% capacity. The dam model’s residuals are not fully white noise (Ljung-Box \(p\) = 0.001), so these intervals are probably too narrow.
  • The raw cross-correlation between dam levels and SST is dominated by their five-to-six-month seasonal offset, not by El Niño. After prewhitening, the strongest lead is at 10 months (CCF = -0.091), with 0 of 12 lead lags outside the 95% bounds.
  • A time series linear model finds a significant negative relationship between lagged SST and dam levels (\(\hat\beta\) = -2.779, \(p\) = 0.012), as hypothesised, but its residuals are strongly autocorrelated. With ARIMA errors, the SST coefficient is -0.341 (\(p\) = 0.663), and the dynamic regression’s forecasts (CRPS = 18.852) are no better than the univariate model’s (18.700).
  • There is no statistically significant evidence that SST at 95°W is a useful monthly leading indicator of Theewaterskloof Dam levels.
  • Forecast rankings depend on the test window: the Naive model beat the ARIMA model during the 2019–2021 post-drought recovery. This is why selection used cross-validation rather than a single test set.
  • Gaps in the buoy record from November 2021 meant that no model could forecast into the 2026/2027 El Niño period.
  • Model logit-transformed capacity so that prediction intervals stay within 0-100%.

What I would do differently next time:

  • Use catchment rainfall and water-use data as predictors, since they drive dam inflow and outflow directly.
  • Use a gap-free ENSO index, such as the Oceanic Niño Index, instead of a single buoy line, so that forecasts could reach 2027.
  • Fit a regime-switching model that allows the SST–dam relationship to differ between El Niño, La Niña and neutral phases.
  • Model logit-transformed capacity so that prediction intervals stay within 0–100%.
